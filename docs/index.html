01 · Implementation

What the program solves

The program solves quasi-static brittle fracture in a two-dimensional isotropic solid. A displacement field describes deformation and a scalar intactness field regularizes a crack over a finite length scale. Tensile elastic energy drives fracture; compressive elastic energy remains undegraded. Accepted load states satisfy mechanical equilibrium, a bound-constrained phase subproblem, irreversibility, and coupled residual criteria.

Phase convention φ = 1 denotes intact material.
φ = 0 denotes fully broken material.
Units are kN and mm with unit out-of-plane thickness.

Physics

Small-strain isotropic elasticity with uniform, analytic-gradient, or region-overridden coefficients; a smoothed spectral split; normalized stiffness degradation; AT2 fracture energy; and maximum-tensile-energy history.

Numerics

CG1 displacement and phase fields, DG0 history, consistent-tangent Newton mechanics, KKT-checked phase minimization, staggered coupling, rollback, and load cutback.

Audit trail

Configuration and material provenance, accepted-state CSV, atomic status summary, vector reactions, force balance, crack-path and energy diagnostics, XDMF/HDF5 fields, PNG plots, and explicit failure reporting.

02 · Mathematical model

Governing equations

Let Ω = [0,W] × [0,H] be the specimen. The primary unknowns are displacement u: Ω → ℝ² and intactness φ: Ω → [0,1]. The history H stores the largest tensile-energy density reached by an accepted load state.

u, v
displacement and admissible displacement test function
φ, q
intactness and scalar phase test function
ε(u)
small-strain tensor
ψ±
tensile and compressive spectral elastic energies
H
cellwise irreversible tensile-energy history
E, ν
Young modulus and Poisson ratio; constants or DG0 spatial fields
Gc, ℓ
fracture toughness and regularization length; constants or DG0 spatial fields
kres
residual stiffness of fully broken material

Kinematics and elastic constants

Small strain
ε(u) = sym ∇u = ½(∇u + ∇uT)
Lamé parameters
μ(x) = E(x) / [2(1+ν(x))]
λplane strain(x) = E(x)ν(x) / [(1+ν(x))(1−2ν(x))],    λplane stress(x) = E(x)ν(x) / (1−ν(x)²)
Built-in linear material profiles
px(x,y) = p0 + (p1−p0)x/W,    py(x,y) = p0 + (p1−p0)y/H

Here p is E, ν, Gc, or ℓ. Ordinary material keywords give the left/bottom values; their *_end partners give the right/top values. File mode also supports constant, linear, affine, box-region, and circular-region definitions.

Smoothed spectral split

For the symmetric 2 × 2 strain tensor, the implementation evaluates the two principal strains analytically. A small smoothing η makes the spectral residual differentiable at zero strain and repeated eigenvalues.

Principal strains and Macaulay parts
d = √[(εxx−εyy)² + 4εxy² + η²],   ε1,2 = ½(tr ε ± d)
⟨z⟩± = ½[z ± √(z²+η²)]
Tensile and compressive energy
ψ±(ε) = ½λ⟨tr ε⟩±2 + μ[⟨ε1⟩±2 + ⟨ε2⟩±2]

Default smoothing: η = 10−10.

Stiffness degradation and energy densities

Normalized degradation
g(φ) = kres + (1−kres)φ²,   g(0)=kres,   g(1)=1
Elastic and AT2 fracture density
Ψe(u,φ) = g(φ)ψ+(ε(u)) + ψ−(ε(u))
Ψf(φ) = Gc[(1−φ)²/(2ℓ) + ℓ|∇φ|²/2]
Instantaneous internal energy reported to CSV
Einst = ∫Ω[Ψe(u,φ)+Ψf(φ)] dΩ

This is a physical snapshot based on the current strain. It is distinct from the history-driven quadratic potential used inside the phase solve.

Mechanical equilibrium

Stress and weak residual
σ(u,φ) = ∂Ψe/∂ε,    Ru(u;v) = ∫Ω σ(u,φ):ε(v) dΩ = 0

The consistent Newton tangent is Ju = ∂Ru/∂u, generated by UFL differentiation. For graded materials, σ uses the local DG0 Lamé coefficients on each cell.

History and phase evolution

Accepted-state history
Hn+1(x) = max[Hn(x), ψ+(ε(un+1(x)))]
Phase weak equation at fixed H
∫Ω{ Gcℓ∇φ·∇q + [Gc/ℓ + 2(1−kres)H]φq }dΩ = ∫Ω(Gc/ℓ)q dΩ

When material data vary, Gc(x) and ℓ(x) are retained inside every integral; they are not replaced by domain averages.

Discrete constrained phase minimization
minimizeφ   ½φTA(H)φ − bTφ   subject to   0 ≤ φ ≤ φn,   φ = 0 on the starter-notch degrees of freedom

The clipped unconstrained solution is only an initial candidate. When its projected gradient exceeds tolerance, L-BFGS-B solves the box-constrained quadratic. Acceptance requires the projected KKT residual to pass.

KKT sign conditions

With r = Aφ−b: r = 0 at free interior degrees of freedom, r ≥ 0 at a lower bound, and r ≤ 0 at an upper bound. Fixed notch degrees of freedom are excluded from the projected residual.

Natural phase boundary

Where no phase value is imposed, integration by parts gives the no-flux condition (Gcℓ∇φ)·n = 0. Because Gcℓ is positive, this is equivalent to ∇φ·n = 0. The internal starter-notch nodes remain fixed at φ = 0.

Response and crack diagnostics

Discrete grip reactions and integrated damage
RΓ,i = Σa∈dofs(Γ,i) [ruunconstrained]a,    D = ∫Ω(1−φ)dΩ

The code sums owned entries of the assembled mechanical residual before essential-condition elimination; the PETSc backend then applies an MPI reduction. Top-plus-bottom sums are reported as force-balance checks.

The reported crack front is the largest x-coordinate in the mesh-connected component of nodes satisfying φ ≤ 0.2 and attached to the starter notch. Crack extension is max(0, xfront−a0). Connectivity prevents isolated damaged islands from being counted as the main crack. Mixed-mode output additionally records the connected tip coordinates, graph path length, kink angle, and connected-node count.

03 · Finite elements

Discretization and assembled systems

FieldSpaceStorage and role
Displacement u[CG1]²Continuous vector-valued linear Lagrange basis on triangles
Intactness φCG1Continuous scalar linear Lagrange basis; nodal bounds enforce no healing
History HDG0One discontinuous constant value per triangle
E, ν, Gc, ℓDG0Static cellwise material coefficients; constant in uniform mode

Mesh

A structured W × H rectangle is divided into nx × ny rectangles and split into 2nxny right triangles.

Resolution measure

The actual largest triangle edge is h = √[(W/nx)²+(H/ny)²]. Strict mode requires h/minΩℓ ≤ 0.5, equivalent to the cellwise rule on this uniform structured mesh.

History representation

CG1 displacement gives cellwise-constant strain on affine triangles, so ψ+ and H are represented once per cell by DG0.

Assembly and linear algebra

DOLFINx creates the mesh and spaces, FFCx compiles the UFL forms, and DOLFINx assembles native sparse matrices and vectors. On native Windows, matrices are converted to SciPy CSR and solved with scipy.sparse.linalg.spsolve. Vector-space BSR matrices are explicitly converted to CSR. A singular, rank-deficient, nonfinite, or nonconvergent solve becomes a clear runtime failure that can trigger load rollback.

Serial execution

This backend requires exactly one MPI rank. The finite-element formulation is independent of that deployment choice, but the included SciPy solve is not a parallel PETSc implementation.

For institute clusters, use the separate Linux PETSc/MPI backend. It uses distributed DOLFINx meshes, PETSc SNES/SNESVI, collective XDMF/HDF5, and supplied launch templates for genuine 16- or 32-rank jobs.

Essential conditions

  • uy = 0 along the bottom edge.
  • ux = 0 at the lower-left vertex to remove horizontal rigid translation.
  • uy = Umaxt along the top edge.
  • φ = 0 at nodes with y = H/2 and x ≤ a0.

The mesh validator requires even ny and integer notch_length / (width / nx), so the notch lies exactly on mesh vertices.

Configurable mixed-mode kinematics

Let Δ(t) = t(Us,Uo), where Uo is max_displacement and Us is the signed max_sliding_displacement. All schemes use the same pseudo-time, so a load cutback scales opening and sliding together and preserves their prescribed ratio.

05 · Nonlinear solution

Transactional staggered algorithm

During an attempted load, the last accepted un, φn, and Hn remain immutable. A trial becomes physical history only after every convergence gate passes.

Accepted state
Target load
u → H → φ
Convergence gates
Commit or rollback
  1. Validate and construct

    Check geometry, boundary scheme, material mode, pointwise material ranges, mesh alignment, local h/ℓ, tolerances, and output controls. Create mesh, DG0 material coefficients, adjacency, function spaces, boundary conditions, UFL forms, and the run manifest.

  2. Initialize the diffuse notch

    Set u = 0, φ = 1, and H = 0; set notch nodes to φ = 0; then solve the constrained phase problem once at zero load to equilibrate the regularized notch profile.

  3. Propose a load target

    Begin with nominal Δt = 1/N and ttrial = min(1,tn+Δt). Snapshot u, φ, H, and active-bound visit counters before applying both opening and sliding values for the selected boundary scheme.

  4. Solve displacement by Newton

    At fixed φ, assemble the nonlinear mechanical residual and consistent tangent. Apply lifted essential conditions, solve a sparse Newton update, update u, and require free residual, increment, and boundary-violation limits.

  5. Rebuild trial history

    Interpolate ψ+(u) into DG0 and assign H = max(Hn,ψ+). Rebuilding from Hn prevents inner solver iterates from becoming fictitious load history.

  6. Solve the phase subproblem

    Assemble A(H)φ = b with local Gc and ℓ coefficients, solve the unconstrained system for a starting candidate, apply bounds 0 ≤ φ ≤ φn, and run L-BFGS-B when the projected KKT residual requires it.

  7. Test coupled equilibrium

    Reassemble the mechanical residual after the phase update. Accept the staggered iterate only when phase increment ≤ 10−5, phase KKT residual ≤ 10−8, and free mechanical residual ≤ 10−6.

  8. Commit the state

    Append one CSV row with opening/sliding values, vector reactions, force balance, and crack-path diagnostics; optionally write XDMF fields; reset the consecutive-cutback count; and advance accepted pseudo-time. Only committed states appear in output histories.

  9. Rollback and adapt when needed

    If Newton, phase optimization, or staggered convergence fails, restore every snapshot, multiply Δt by 0.5, and retry from the same accepted state. Opening and sliding remain proportional to the retried pseudo-time.

    A reduced increment grows one level toward nominal only after three consecutive accepted steps with no cutback and at most 10 staggered iterations. More than eight consecutive cutbacks produces an explicit terminal failure and an auditable partial summary.
  10. Finalize

    Close field files, rewrite the accepted-state CSV, generate plots, and atomically replace summary.json with completed status. Post-processing errors are also captured as failed status.

Newton defaults

  • 25 maximum Newton updates
  • 10−8 relative residual
  • 10−10 absolute residual
  • 10−10 increment / BC limit

Phase defaults

  • 10−8 projected KKT limit
  • 200 optimizer iterations
  • 10−15 optimizer function tolerance
  • 40 line-search steps

Load defaults

  • 60 nominal increments
  • 0.5 cutback factor
  • 8 maximum consecutive cutbacks

06 · Line-oriented configuration

LAMMPS-style input files

An input file is a readable sequence of keyword–value commands. Every SimulationConfig field is available, so complete studies do not require editing Python. Parsing is deliberately strict and fails before an expensive finite-element solve.

Grammar

keywordvalue# optional comment
  • Use exactly one keyword and one scalar value per nonempty line.
  • Keywords are case-insensitive and use the field names listed below.
  • Blank lines and lines beginning with # are ignored.
  • An unquoted # begins an inline comment.
  • Quote string values containing spaces or # with single or double quotes.
  • Boolean values accept true/false, yes/no, on/off, or 1/0, case-insensitively.
  • Integer fields require integer tokens; floating fields accept decimal or scientific notation and must be finite.
  • Each keyword may appear at most once, including case variants.
  • An input file containing only blanks and comments is rejected.
  • There is no separate run keyword; invoking the Python command parses, validates, and runs the simulation.
  • Expressions, variables, environment expansion, line continuation, nested includes, and unit conversion are not supported.

Spatial material modes

Built-in gradients

linear_x interpolates every material property from its ordinary value at x=0 to its *_end value at x=W. linear_y does the same from y=0 to y=H. Uniform mode ignores the end values. 

material_mode linear_x
young_modulus 210.0
young_modulus_end 105.0
fracture_toughness 2.7e-3
fracture_toughness_end 1.8e-3

File-defined regions

File mode combines whole-domain profiles with prioritized box or circle overrides. The coefficients are evaluated at cell centers and stored as DG0 fields. 

material_mode file
material_file materials/inclusion.material
write_material_fields true
profile | region | overridecommand arguments# optional comment
Material-file grammar and inclusion
profile PROPERTY constant VALUE
profile PROPERTY linear_x LEFT RIGHT
profile PROPERTY linear_y BOTTOM TOP
profile PROPERTY affine ORIGIN DX DY

region NAME box XMIN XMAX YMIN YMAX PRIORITY
region NAME circle CX CY RADIUS PRIORITY
override NAME PROPERTY VALUE

# Example
profile young_modulus constant 210.0
profile poisson_ratio constant 0.30
profile fracture_toughness constant 2.7e-3
profile length_scale constant 0.150
region stiff_inclusion circle 0.65 0.50 0.12 20
override stiff_inclusion young_modulus 420.0

PROPERTY is young_modulus, poisson_ratio, fracture_toughness, or length_scale. An affine profile is p(x,y)=ORIGIN+DX·x+DY·y. Higher-priority regions win where regions overlap, and priorities must be unique. Positive/range checks are applied to every cell, and strict resolution uses the local ℓ. Curved interfaces are cell-midpoint stair-step approximations unless the mesh is refined.

Commands/properties are case-insensitive. At most one explicit profile is allowed per property; an omitted profile inherits the ordinary main-input value as a constant. Region names and integer priorities are unique, and override pairs cannot be duplicated. Blank lines and # comments are allowed.

Strict error handling

Rejected during parsing

  • Missing or unreadable UTF-8 input file
  • Unknown keyword, with a close-name suggestion when available
  • Duplicate keyword and first-occurrence line number
  • Missing or extra argument
  • Invalid integer, float, boolean, or empty string
  • NaN or infinite floating-point value

Rejected during validation or construction

  • Invalid physical range or solver tolerance
  • Unknown boundary scheme, or sliding with the legacy scheme
  • Missing or malformed material file, profile, region, or override
  • Empty material region or nonphysical cellwise coefficient
  • Odd ny or notch/grid misalignment
  • Under-resolved local h/ℓ in strict mode
  • Invalid cutback or load-growth controls
  • Invalid output cadence

Main-input syntax and scalar configuration errors include the resolved path and line number and exit before finite-element construction. Material-file, region-cell, and pointwise coefficient checks complete while the DG0 fields are constructed and still fail before the nonlinear solve begins. See INPUT_FILE.md and phasefield_input.py for the standalone reference and parser implementation.

CSV column groups

Load and response

step, pseudo-time, opening_displacement, sliding_displacement, four top/bottom x/y reactions, two force-balance checks, energies, damage, and maximum history. Compatibility aliases remain displacement=opening_displacement and reaction_force=top_reaction_y.

Crack and constraints

Minimum phase, connected front/extension, crack_tip_x/y, graph path length, kink angle, connected-node count, phase KKT and increment, optimizer iterations, and active bounds.

Convergence and adaptivity

Staggered/Newton counts, residuals, increment, boundary violation, history increment, load increment, cutbacks, cumulative active-bound visits.

Rejected trials are not output states

Rollback restores fields and cumulative counters before a smaller load is attempted. A terminal failure leaves CSV and summary data for the last accepted state and closes any open XDMF files.

12 · Scope

Limitations and extensions

  • History-field approximation. The maximum-history formulation is an engineering approximation to the exact global irreversibility variational inequality, although each implemented phase subproblem enforces nodal bounds and KKT optimality.
  • Diffuse starter notch. An internal φ = 0 line represents the flaw; the mesh contains no traction-free geometric slit.
  • Two-dimensional split. The principal-strain model is explicitly 2D. Plane stress changes λ; it does not create a three-dimensional state.
  • Two solver backends. This page's native-Windows SciPy backend is serial. The separate Linux PETSc/MPI backend supports partitioned 16/32-rank cluster runs, subject to allocation-specific MPI and parallel-I/O validation.
  • Resolution gate. h/ℓ ≤ 0.5 is a minimum discretization rule, not a mesh-convergence proof. Peak load, energy, and crack path still need refinement studies.
  • Quasi-static load control. Cutback improves robustness but is not arc-length continuation. Severe snap-back can require energy control or an arc-length method.
  • Crack-front threshold. φ ≤ 0.2 and mesh connectivity define a diagnostic only; they do not enter the evolution law.
  • Mode-mix interpretation. The prescribed sliding/opening ratio is not KII/KI. Quantitative mode mixity requires a separate near-tip or interaction-integral calculation.
  • Split and contact. Mixed-mode crack paths depend on the chosen spectral tension/compression split. The model has no separate frictional crack-face contact law, so closed-face shear transfer needs particular care.
  • Spatial coefficients. Analytic gradients and material regions are stored as DG0 cellwise constants. Non-mesh-fitted interfaces and curved inclusions are midpoint-classified stair steps; material contrast can therefore expose mesh orientation bias.
  • Higher-order spaces. DG0 history matches cellwise-constant strain for affine CG1 triangles. Higher-order displacement requires a quadrature history or a documented projection.
  • No experimental calibration. The demonstration establishes reproducible numerical behavior, not predictive material validation.
